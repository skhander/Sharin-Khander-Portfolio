Build a premium product portfolio website for **Sharin Khander**, a product builder and former engineer.

### The goal

The primary audience is a **product hiring manager or product leader**.

They should land on the site and immediately understand:

* Sharin is a **product builder**, not a traditional PM who only writes specs.
* She builds **habit-forming consumer products people love to use**.
* She has deep experience in **marketplaces, AI-powered discovery, ecommerce, checkout, personalization, virtual try-on, and consumer productivity**.
* She thinks deeply about **what to build, why to build it, and how to make it feel great**.
* She can move from strategy → product thinking → prototype → shipped experience.

The site should make me want to **see the work**.

Do NOT make this feel like a generic PM portfolio, résumé website, SaaS landing page, or personal-brand site.

It should feel like a **beautiful product archive**.

---

# Visual direction

Think **minimal, editorial, elevated, modern consumer tech**.

References in spirit:

* Apple product pages
* Linear
* Arc
* Stripe
* high-end fashion editorial
* great product design portfolios

But do not copy any of them.

### Design principles

* Mostly black, white, and neutral tones.
* One restrained accent color used sparingly for interaction, links, tags, or highlights.
* Lots of whitespace.
* Strong typography.
* Excellent hierarchy.
* Large headlines.
* Short paragraphs.
* Tight copy.
* Generous margins.
* Avoid visual clutter.
* Avoid unnecessary cards, pills, gradients, shadows, or decorative UI.
* Avoid excessive rounded corners.
* Avoid generic "AI portfolio" aesthetics.
* Avoid stock photography.
* Let the actual product screenshots be the visual centerpiece.

The website should feel **quiet and confident**.

Whitespace should communicate confidence, not emptiness.

# Project framework

Every project should include these sections:

### 01 — The Problem

What wasn't working?

Keep this to 2–4 sentences.

Focus on the actual user or business problem.

Show supporting product screenshots when useful.

---

### 02 — The Insight

What did Sharin learn that changed the direction of the product?

This is important.

Don't just explain what the team built.

Show **how she thinks**.

Include:

* user research
* behavioral data
* competitive/contextual insights
* product principles
* constraints

Use large pull quotes or metrics when appropriate.

---

### 03 — The Bet

What did Sharin decide to do?

Explain the product decision clearly.

Use concise language:

**We believed X.
So we built Y.
Because Z.**

This should communicate product judgment without turning into a strategy deck.

---

### 04 — The Work

This is the visual centerpiece.

Give the product room.

Support:

* large screenshots
* image galleries
* videos
* prototypes
* Figma embeds if possible
* interactive demos
* before / after comparisons
* annotated screens

Screens should be much larger than the surrounding text.

Allow images to break outside the normal text column when appropriate.

---

### 05 — How I Made Decisions

This section should show actual product thinking.

Examples:

**Decision:** Put more emphasis on imagery.

**Why:** The inventory is the product. Shoppers need to evaluate condition, material, fit, and details quickly.

Or:

**Decision:** Start with women's apparel.

**Why:** It gave the team a focused initial surface to validate model precision before expanding.

Keep these sections concise.

Do not create generic "Challenges / Solutions / Results" filler.

---

### 06 — Outcome

Show measurable impact when available.

Use large typography for important metrics.

Example:

**+1.1%**
visitor conversion

**+1.5%**
PDP views

Then explain the context in one sentence.

If quantitative results aren't available, use a qualitative outcome such as:

* launched to X% of users
* reduced operational effort
* enabled editorial teams to scale
* expanded the product's capabilities

Never fabricate metrics.

---

### 07 — What I'd Do Next

Optional.

Use this only when it adds real insight.

Show that Sharin thinks beyond launch:

> If I had another quarter, I'd focus on...

Keep it short.

# Interaction design

The site should feel like a product someone wants to explore.

Add subtle interactions:

* smooth page transitions
* tasteful hover states
* image zoom
* sticky project navigation
* scroll progress
* video playback when appropriate
* subtle motion
* expandable sections

But **do not over-animate**.

No distracting particle effects.

No excessive parallax.

No gimmicky cursor.

No animation that gets in the way of reading or viewing the work.

---

# Typography

Typography is extremely important.

Use a modern sans-serif with excellent typography.

Possible choices:

* Inter
* Geist
* SF Pro-style system stack

Use:

* very large headlines
* tight tracking on large type
* comfortable body copy
* strong contrast between headings and supporting text

The site should remain highly readable.

Avoid tiny text.

---

# Layout

Use a strong editorial grid.

Desktop:

* generous margins
* max-width content container
* wide visual canvas
* readable text column
* images can extend wider than text

Mobile:

* preserve hierarchy
* make screenshots easy to view
* avoid tiny UI
* maintain generous spacing
* make navigation simple

# Most important product principle

**The portfolio itself should demonstrate Sharin's product taste.**

Do not explain that she has good product taste.

Make the website feel like something she built.

The actual work should dominate the experience.

A hiring manager should be able to skim the site in 60 seconds and understand:

**Who is she? → What has she built? → How does she think? → What did she ship? → What happened?**

Then they should be able to spend 10–15 minutes going deep into the projects that interest them.

Prioritize **clarity, taste, hierarchy, and the work itself** over clever portfolio conventions.
