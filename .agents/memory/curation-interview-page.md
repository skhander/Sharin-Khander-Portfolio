The Curation Tool page is meant to be glanced at during an interview so the owner can reconstruct a spoken story from eight short beats. Do not turn it back into a dense, linear case study.

**Why:** The user explicitly asked for an executive-presentation/interview-cheat-sheet format with strong memory anchors and minimal copy.

**How to apply:** When editing this page, keep the progression from problem to product shift, ownership, recommendation-versus-taste challenge, outcome, and future agents/human judgment. Treat 2× onsite edits as supplied by the user; do not infer a 2× GMV claim from it. Keep metrics distinct from faster creation time.

Keep the problem statement and product-shift cue understated, in sentence case and the existing pink portfolio palette rather than giant all-caps hero slogans.

**Why:** The user explicitly corrected both the oversized “Weeks of Work. Days Live.” treatment and the oversized all-caps “From finding products → curating” treatment, asking for modest editorial text aligned with the homepage.

**How to apply:** Preserve the interview beats and supporting numbers, but do not promote either cue into an attention-grabbing headline when refining this page.

In the problem beat, present assortment production time against shelf-life as a comparison, not as unrelated volume or duration statistics.

**Why:** The user clarified that the contrast between how long an assortment takes to produce and how briefly it stays live is the point they need to remember in an interview.

**How to apply:** Keep those two measures visibly paired and clearly labeled whenever the opening metrics are rearranged.

The whole Curation Tool page should follow the homepage's visual language, not a separate dark or presentation-style theme. Continuity must be obvious immediately after opening the homepage card, not only after scrolling.

**Why:** The user repeatedly objected that entering the detail page felt like entering a different site. They chose an actual curated-assortment image over repeating the homepage card's company, title, and subtitle, then moved the image from hero position into the first beat.

**How to apply:** Use the supplied assortment visual as smaller supporting context in the problem beat near the production-time comparison, not as a full-width hero or a reason to restore the repeated card title block. Use the homepage's white canvas, Manrope, charcoal text, thin rules, and restrained existing burgundy, pink, and olive accents across all eight beats while keeping text selectable and media functional.

In the Goals beat, indicate the direction of production time and edit volume with plain arrows, not decorative mini bar charts.

**Why:** The user found a descending-bars glyph beside production time distracting and specifically asked to remove it while keeping the directional cues.

**How to apply:** Keep the goals easy to scan without implying charted data where no values are shown.
