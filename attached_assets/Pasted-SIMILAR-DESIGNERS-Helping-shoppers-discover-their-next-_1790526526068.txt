The problem
80% of shoppers bounced from Designer pages.
We believed they weren't finding enough products that resonated—and needed better paths to keep exploring.

WHAT I BUILT
A recommendation system that evolved from “customers also shop” into a personalized way to discover designers based on how they look, feel, and fit your budget.

That progression is the coolest part of the story. Show it visually. Three cards getting progressively smarter is much more compelling than explaining the methodology in a paragraph.

THE HARD PART
Our first version was technically reasonable—but not particularly useful.

A quick UX study told us that obvious pairings like Louis Vuitton → Celine weren't giving shoppers a new path to explore. Leadership wanted to test it anyway, so we did. The result: people clicked, but didn't go deeper or convert.

Then:

We learned that similarity wasn't enough. People wanted alternatives that felt like them—including designers at a lower price point.

WHAT HAPPENED

PHASE 01
Generic recommendations
→ No harm. No lift.

PHASE 02
Visual + attribute similarity
→ More PDP views + ATC, but no conversion lift

PHASE 03
Personalized + price-aware recommendations
→ +3% GMV / visitor
→ −10% designer-page bounce

WHAT I LEARNED
“Similar” isn't a model output. It's a product decision.

You had to define what similar actually meant, decide what signals mattered, determine what quality bar was good enough to ship, and then learn from real behavior.

One thing I'd change from your original story

I wouldn't lead with “ML” even though the ML work is impressive.

Lead with:

We wanted to help people discover their next favorite designer.

Then let the sophistication reveal itself:

behavior → visual → attributes → personalization → price

That makes the project immediately understandable to someone who has never heard of TRR, while still signaling to a ShopMy interviewer: this person understands recommendations, knows how to work with ML, prototypes herself, and actually ships.
