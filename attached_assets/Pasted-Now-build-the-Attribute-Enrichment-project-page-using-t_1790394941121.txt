Now build the **Attribute Enrichment** project page using the same editorial, visual storytelling approach as Elevated Experience.

The story should be **concise, punchy, and highly scannable**. The product visuals should do most of the explaining. Avoid turning this into a long AI case study.

The core story is:

**6M listings. 12K new products every day. Not enough structure to understand them. We used LLMs to turn product images into a new layer of fashion metadata—and tested where that metadata actually created value.**

---

# 1. The problem

Start with a large statement:

> **Our catalog knew what a product was. It didn't know what it felt like.**

Then show a product example with the new attributes visually attached to it.

Keep the supporting copy to 2–3 sentences:

> We had 6M live listings and added 12K new products every day. But shoppers couldn't search naturally by trend, occasion, or vibe—and those signals weren't powering recommendations, personalization, or curation.

Use the numbers as large visual anchors:

**6M**
live listings

**12K**
new products / day

# 2. The idea

Headline:

> **Give every product a richer understanding of style.**

Show the product → attributes transformation visually.

Example:

Then one sentence:

> We used LLMs to predict fashion attributes from product images and tag the entire catalog.

# 3. Why it mattered

Then one sharp line:

# 5. The hard part wasn't generating attributes. It was making them usable.

This should be the key product-thinking section.

Headline:

> **AI could generate the attributes. We had to make them product-ready.**

Show examples of messy output / normalization.

Then three concise trade-offs:

### Normalize the language

AI returned variations like capitalization and inconsistent values. We needed a controlled taxonomy.

### Structure the data

Filters couldn't rely on free-form text. Attributes needed to be structured and predictable.

### Protect the brand

Some outputs were technically plausible but simply weren't "brand right." We needed a way to catch and remove slop.

Keep the visual treatment simple. Don't explain the entire taxonomy.

---

# 6. We didn't test everything at once.

Make the experimentation strategy visual.

Show a horizontal progression:

**Search → Browse → Recommendations**

Then separately:

**Curation Tool ↔ Search**

Supporting copy:

> Search was the largest surface, so it was the first proving ground. Recommendations followed. In parallel, we built Curation Tools with the merchandising team to prove the operational value.

---

# 7. The Curation Tool was the unlock.

Make this a major visual moment.

Headline:

> **What if a merchandiser could build a trend lookbook with one search?**

Show the actual Curation Tool interface.

Then:

> Enter a trend. Get a programmatic assortment. Delete the slop.

This should communicate the workflow visually rather than explaining it in a paragraph.

Optional supporting line:

> The goal was to give merchandising a scalable way to create trend-based assortments without manually tagging thousands of products.

---

# 8. The first test didn't work.

This is important. Do NOT hide the failed experiment.

Headline:

> **The baseline test didn't move.**

Then:

> We learned something important: shoppers weren't naturally searching this way yet.

Show a simple search example:

**"quiet luxury"**
**"date night"**
**"boho"**
**"what should I wear to..."**

Then:

> The capability existed. The behavior didn't.

This is the product insight.

---

# 9. The measurement problem

Make the nuance clear without overexplaining.

Headline:

> **You can't measure a behavior people don't know exists.**

Supporting copy:

> Natural-language search required a stronger go-to-market strategy to teach shoppers they could search by trend, occasion, and vibe. That meant we couldn't expect a controlled test to capture the full impact.

Then:

**Pre → Post**

> We shifted toward measuring the change in behavior after launch rather than relying on a single A/B test.

Do not imply that the pre/post result proves causality.

---

# 10. Where the value showed up

End with the strongest quantitative result.

Huge metric:

**+10% Conversion**

Small label:

**Product recommendations**

Then:

> Recommendations showed the largest step-change lift, suggesting richer product understanding could create value even when shoppers weren't explicitly searching for these attributes.

Keep this wording appropriately cautious.

---

# Final takeaway

End with one large statement:

> **The real opportunity wasn't adding more metadata. It was giving The RealReal a richer language for understanding style.**

Then a small supporting line:

> One foundation. Multiple product experiences: search, recommendations, browse, personalization, and curation.
