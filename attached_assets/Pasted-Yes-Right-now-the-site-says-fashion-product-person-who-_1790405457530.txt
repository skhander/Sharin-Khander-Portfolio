Yes. **Right now the site says “fashion product person who has good taste.”** The visual language is editorial: serif typography, paper, gold foil, muted pastels, art direction.

What’s missing is **evidence of making things**.

I would *not* turn it into a stereotypical “tech builder” portfolio with dark mode, grids, code, terminal windows, etc. That would fight the fashion sensibility. Instead:

> **Fashion editorial on the surface. Product-building artifacts underneath.**

Think **fashion magazine × product studio × prototype wall.**

### 1. Make the hero literally look like something you built

Your pink **“Product builder.”** card is currently just a statement.

Make it an **artifact**.

For example, keep the beautiful pink paper, but layer little pieces of product work into it:

```text
PRODUCT BUILDER.
─────────────────────
I build better ways to shop.

        ┌───────────────┐
        │ Search        │
        │ "90s black    │
        │ tie dress"    │
        └───────────────┘
              ↓
        ┌───────────────┐
        │ ML            │
        │ recommendations│
        └───────────────┘
              ↓
        ┌───────────────┐
        │ Discovery     │
        └───────────────┘

7 zero → one products
150+ experiments
$340M GMV impact
```

Not literally this UI—but **a little ecosystem of things you've made**.

The key is that the viewer should think:

**“Oh, this person actually builds product.”**

rather than:

**“This person calls themselves a product builder.”**

## 2. Introduce a second visual language: “construction”

You already have **fashion materials**:

* gold foil
* torn paper
* editorial serif
* soft pink
* olive
* cream

Now introduce **builder materials**:

* thin technical rules
* grids
* numbered annotations
* little diagrams
* arrows
* system maps
* interface fragments
* cropped prototypes
* experiment labels
* handwritten/typed annotations
* tiny monospace metadata

So you get this tension:

**EDITORIAL**

> Sharin Khander
> Trend, Vibe, Occasion Discovery

**BUILDER**

> 01 / PROBLEM
> 02 / HYPOTHESIS
> 03 / PROTOTYPE
> 04 / SHIP
> 05 / MEASURE

That's much more distinctive than simply putting “AI” or “Product” everywhere.

# 7. I'd also change the visual treatment of your categories

You currently have:

**ALL / DISCOVERY / COMMERCE / AI**

That's very portfolio-site.

Instead, make them feel more like **what you build**:

**ALL · DISCOVERY · SHOPPING · AI**

And potentially introduce:

**0→1 · ML · EXPERIMENTS**

Not necessarily as filters. They can be little tags attached to projects.

For example:

### Trend, Vibe, Occasion Discovery

`DISCOVERY` `ML` `0→1`

That communicates your range without saying:

> “I am a multidisciplinary product builder with deep expertise…”

Which is exactly the kind of portfolio copy that gets ignored.

# The aesthetic formula I'd use

Your existing design already has **80% of the right DNA**.

I'd think of the system as:

### 70% fashion editorial

* serif typography
* cream
* pink
* olive
* gold
* whitespace
* beautiful imagery
* tactile paper

### 30% product studio

* grids
* diagrams
* interface fragments
* technical annotations
* numbered systems
* experiment data
* prototypes
* arrows
* “built / shipped / tested”

**Don't make the 30% look like Silicon Valley.**

Make it look like **a fashion editor's desk covered in product prototypes.**

That's the visual identity I think fits *you* unusually well: **someone who understands fashion deeply enough to have taste, but is technical enough to actually build the systems underneath it.**
